Character sheet

Rani · रानी

Short for maharani. She's an original character who borrows motifs from the references without copying any of their women. She has to look like the same girl in all five styles, so her identity rests on a few fixed traits that survive any style change.

Fixed traits (never change)

  • Face: early 20s, warm medium-brown skin, thick straight brows, heavy-lidded eyes with a sharp winged liner, deep berry lips, a small red bindi, a tiny gold nose stud on the left
  • Hair: long, wavy and black with a side part. One thin braid on the left, tied with a red-and-gold paranda tassel
  • Heart sunglasses: pink, heart-shaped, usually pushed up on her head
  • Hands: bridal-style mehendi on both hands, neon-yellow almond nails, a gold bangle stack on the left wrist only
  • Charm: a tiny nimbu-mirchi charm hanging from her phone

Outfits

  • Main (most shots): a hot-pink cropped blouse with marigold paisley, a green-and-red bandhani dupatta over one shoulder, flared dark denim with white embroidered hems, red juttis
  • Sleep (shots 4–5, 25): an oversized marigold tee printed "कल से", striped pyjamas, sunglasses still on her head
  • Maharani (shots 15–16): a red saree with a wide gold border, a statement maang tikka, jhumkas, the same bangles

Expressions and poses

  • Seedhi shakal: wide innocent eyes, a tiny smile
  • Side-eye: lids half down, eyes to the side, lips pressed
  • Wink: one eye shut, sunglasses tipped down
  • Smug: chin up, one brow raised
  • Drenched: mouth open, hair flattened, sunglasses fogged
  • Signature poses: palm to camera, holding a chai glass with her pinky out, pointing, arms crossed

Motion plan

How it moves

The look is a printed poster coming to life rather than smooth cartoon animation. Small, punchy movements, everything cut to the beat, and the text does most of the dancing. The track is about 114 BPM (my estimate), so one beat lasts about 0.53 seconds, or 12–13 frames at 24 fps.

1 · Make the stills

Generate every shot as a still image using the master prompt plus that shot's style line. Keep her off-centre so there's space for the lyrics. Ask for no text in the image.

2 · Animate the character

Put each still through an image-to-video tool (for example Kling, Runway or Veo) for 3–5 second clips. Ask for small movements only: a blink, a wink, hair sway, the palm pushing forward, the chai glass tilting. Big movements break her face.

3 · Add the poster feel

In the editor, export or conform the clips to 12 fps (animating "on twos"), which gives a hand-made, stop-motion feel. Add a paper-grain overlay and a halftone overlay on every shot, and a slight 1–2 pixel jitter so it looks printed.

4 · Lyrics, camera and cuts

Do all text, camera moves and transitions in CapCut, After Effects or Premiere, cut to the beat. Never ask the AI video tool for text.

Motion language

Floating confetti

Nimbu-mirchis, chillies, hearts, chai glasses, marigolds and sparkles drift behind every shot and pulse on the beat. They tie the five styles together.

Lyrics that move

Lines build one word per half-beat, then keep waving like a flag. Big lines arc like banners, and in the rain shot the words sit on cloud tags.

Steam ribbons

A ribbon of chai steam draws itself across the frame and the lyric rides along it (shots 6, 7 and 10).

Colour snaps

In the chorus, Rani stays put while the background snaps to a new reference colour on every beat, which keeps the energy up without cuts.

Slides, not cuts

Verse shots slide in from the side and characters enter from the edge of the frame, so the video flows like one long moving poster.

Kaleidoscope drops

The instrumental break (shot 12) and the slow cloud line (shot 5) turn into spinning mirrored mandalas with rings pulsing out on the beat.

Production notes

  1. Add lyrics in the edit (CapCut, After Effects or Premiere), never in AI-generated frames. AI image tools still get Devanagari wrong.
  2. These frames use other artists' work. The references are for direction only, and ref 4 carries the artist's "imani.prnt" watermark. For the published video, make original art in these styles, or get each artist's permission and credit them.
  3. One heroine. Rani appears in every shot. Use the master prompt and the turnaround sheet for every generation, so she stays the same girl across all five styles.
  4. The outro is very short. The track ends at about 1:31 and the spoken outro gets roughly 3 seconds. Consider adding a 3–4 second tail of room tone or a beat loop so the "cancel" stamp and the end card have room to breathe.
  5. Food-delivery app (shot 21): show a generic orange app with no real logo, so the video doesn't use another brand's marks.
  6. Shorts and Reels: keep the chorus shots (13–17) centred so they crop cleanly to 9:16.
  7. Thumbnail: use shot 20's ref 2 palm on hot pink, with a huge halftone "अलग".
